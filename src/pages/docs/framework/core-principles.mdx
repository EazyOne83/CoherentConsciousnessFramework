## Core Principles

The CCF rests on seven foundational axioms. These are not arbitrary — each is grounded in systems science, cognitive science, or organizational theory.

## P1 · Coherence is Relational, Not Intrinsic

No system is coherent in isolation. Coherence is always a property of the relationship between a system and its context — between parts and their whole.

## P2 · Coherence Operates Across Scales

The same coherence dynamics appear at individual, relational, collective, and institutional levels. Each scale has its own mechanisms, but the structural patterns are isomorphic.

## P3 · Incoherence is Information

Disruptions, conflicts, and contradictions are not merely dysfunction — they are signals. A coherence-aware system treats incoherence as diagnostic data.

## P4 · Coherence is Dynamic, Not Static

A coherent system is not a frozen one. It is a system that continuously re-integrates perturbations into a stable-yet-adaptive pattern. Rigidity is not coherence.

## P5 · Meaning-Making is the Engine of Coherence

Coherence is sustained through shared interpretation. When agents share a meaning-making frame, coordination becomes low-cost. When frames diverge, coherence collapses.

## P6 · Structure and Culture Must Co-Evolve

At institutional scales, structural design and cultural norms must remain in productive alignment. A mismatch between formal rules and informal norms is a primary source of institutional incoherence.

## P7 · Generative Tension Enables Growth

Optimal coherence is not the absence of tension — it is the right relationship with tension. Too little tension produces stagnation; too much produces fragmentation. The framework maps the viable range.
