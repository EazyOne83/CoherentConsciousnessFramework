## Consciousness Layers

This page explores the first two layers of the CCF stack in depth: **Individual Coherence (L1)** and **Relational Coherence (L2)**. These are the layers most directly concerned with conscious experience.

## L1 — Individual Coherence

The individual layer is the foundation of all other coherence. Without it, higher-layer coherence is unstable — a coherent institution cannot be built from incoherent individuals, even if structural incentives push toward coordination.

### Components of L1 Coherence

**Self-model stability**
A stable self-model is not a rigid one. It is a model that can update without fragmenting — integrating new information while preserving a continuous sense of agency and identity.

**Attentional coherence**
The alignment of attention with stated intention. An individual whose attention is systematically captured by stimuli misaligned with their goals is attentionally incoherent, regardless of stated commitments.

**Interoceptive alignment**
The degree to which somatic signals (body states) are integrated into cognition and decision-making. Chronic disconnection between body and cognition is a primary driver of L1 incoherence.

**Temporal coherence**
The ability to hold past, present, and future in productive relation — making decisions that reflect continuity of purpose across time.

## L2 — Relational Coherence

Relational coherence emerges in the space *between* individuals. It is not the property of either party, but of their interaction.

### The Intersubjective Field

When two agents are in genuine dialogue, a shared attentional space forms. The CCF calls this the **intersubjective field**. When it is coherent:

- Information flows with low loss
- Differences are generative rather than fragmenting
- Each party's self-model is enriched by contact with the other's

When it is incoherent:

- Communication becomes high-cost and low-yield
- Positions harden
- Each party's model of the other diverges from reality

### Mutual Modeling Depth

L2 coherence scales with the depth of mutual modeling:

| Level | Description |
|---|---|
| 0 | No model of the other |
| 1 | Model of other's stated positions |
| 2 | Model of other's reasoning and values |
| 3 | Model of other's model of you |

Most organizational communication operates at Level 1. Deep collaboration requires Level 2–3.
