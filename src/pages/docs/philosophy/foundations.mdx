## Philosophical Foundations

The CCF draws from several intellectual traditions without being reducible to any single one. This page maps the key lineages and how they contribute to the framework.

## Systems Theory & Cybernetics

From **Norbert Wiener**, **Gregory Bateson**, and **Heinz von Foerster**, the framework inherits:

- The centrality of **feedback** as the mechanism of self-regulation
- The concept of **second-order observation** — systems that observe themselves observing
- **Information as difference that makes a difference** (Bateson)

Second-order cybernetics in particular shapes how the CCF treats institutional self-awareness: an organization that cannot model its own coherence state is operating blind.

## Enactivism & Embodied Cognition

From **Varela, Thompson & Rosch** (*The Embodied Mind*) and **Evan Thompson**, the framework takes:

- Cognition as **sense-making**, not computation
- The **organism-environment** coupling as the primary unit of analysis
- Consciousness as **autopoietic** — self-producing, boundary-maintaining

This is why L1 (Individual Coherence) is grounded in the body and affect, not purely in cognition.

## Complexity Science

From the **Santa Fe Institute** tradition (Gell-Mann, Holland, Kauffman):

- **Emergence** — coherent patterns arise from local interactions without central control
- **Fitness landscapes** — coherence is always relative to an environment
- **Edge of chaos** — the most generative zone sits between order and disorder

## Phenomenology

From **Husserl**, **Merleau-Ponty**, and **Heidegger**:

- The **lived body** as the ground of meaning
- **Intentionality** — consciousness is always consciousness *of* something
- **Being-in-the-world** as the irreducible context of any coherence claim

## What the CCF Does NOT Claim

The framework is explicitly **agnostic** on:

- The hard problem of consciousness (whether subjective experience can be physically explained)
- Panpsychism or idealism
- Any specific neuroscientific reduction

It is a **functional and relational** framework — concerned with coherence as an observable, measurable, and improvable property of systems.
