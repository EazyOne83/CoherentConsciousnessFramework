## Glossary

Precise language is foundational to coherent thinking. This glossary defines key terms as they are used specifically within the CCF. Where a term has a broader usage in the literature, the CCF-specific sense is noted.

---

**Autopoiesis**
The property of a system to continuously produce and maintain its own organizational boundary. In the CCF, used to characterize both biological organisms and, by extension, coherent institutions. (Origin: Maturana & Varela)

**Coherence**
The dynamic condition in which components of a system maintain meaningful, adaptive relationships — enabling information flow, coordinated action, and stable identity across perturbations. Distinct from *uniformity* or *consensus*.

**Distributed Sensemaking**
A collective process by which groups construct shared interpretations of ambiguous or complex situations without relying on a single authoritative perspective.

**Enactivism**
A philosophical position holding that cognition arises from the dynamic interaction between an agent and its environment — not from internal representations alone. Central to the CCF's treatment of L1 (Individual Coherence).

**Generative Tension**
The productive state of holding opposing forces (certainty/uncertainty, structure/openness) without collapsing into either. Distinguished from *destructive conflict* (fragmentation) and *false harmony* (rigidity).

**Institutional Coherence (L4)**
The highest-scale layer of the CCF. Describes the alignment between an institution's formal structures, informal norms, stated values, and enacted behaviors over time.

**Intersubjective Field**
The shared attentional and meaning-making space that forms between two or more agents in genuine dialogue. The primary site of Relational Coherence (L2).

**Mutual Modeling**
The cognitive process by which agents build and update models of each other's perspective, intention, and mental state. A prerequisite for sustained Relational Coherence.

**Second-Order Cybernetics**
The study of systems that observe themselves — cybernetics applied to the observer. Influences the CCF's treatment of institutional self-awareness and reflexive governance.

**Signal Fidelity**
The degree to which information is accurately preserved as it crosses an interface (between layers, between agents, or between organizational units). Low signal fidelity is a primary diagnostic indicator of incoherence.
